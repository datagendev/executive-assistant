# The most important GTM Ops skill that you can have right now.

### And no, it's not using Claude Code.

[![Crissy Saunders - CS2's avatar](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pdlH!,w_36,h_36,c_fill,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1d6c6312-cbf4-4498-b7c4-b79a803b1110_400x400.jpeg)](https://substack.com/@crissysaunderscs2)

One of the things Charlie and I have both been very conscious of when creating AI content is focusing on what is actually practical for GTM teams.

I see many people showcasing use cases that are either not feasible for organizations that care about governance and the integrity of their systems or sharing builds and processes that are overly complicated for the value they provide to the business.

And to me, it just looks like a ton of tech debt waiting to be unraveled by someone else (likely our team, haha) in the future.

Unless someone has been in GTM Ops for a while, they likely do not realize the downstream effects of what it means to build and ship something to the GTM team.

GTM Operators and Engineers are essentially engineering “products” for different parts of the GTM team. When you deliver a product, you are setting yourself up to maintain it, enable the team on it, troubleshoot it, and serve as the de facto customer support for it.

Yes, GTM AI can save the GTM team a lot of time, but that does not automatically mean it is saving time for operators. AI is helping GTM Ops build and create things faster, but we are doing more of it, which means more to maintain.

And that time saved is now still consumed by GTM Ops maintaining new products, while also figuring out what they should be working on and how to do it in a way that does not just create a sensitive, unwieldy beast to keep control over.

The real expertise of a GTM Operator right now is not knowing how to use the tools or do cool stuff with Claude Code. Knowing the technical parts of ops has always been only a small part of the job when done well.

The real sign of expertise is being able to find the balance between using AI to make what you already do more efficient and building new things that actually help the GTM team without adding a mountain of tech debt.

But it is getting even harder when requests keep coming in from the GTM team, and even from the C-suite, to build something, adopt a ton of new tools, or just “use AI” without any real strategy behind it. Those requests are from folks who do not always understand the problem I am describing. They have historically seen GTM Ops as doers and technical builders, and they now see AI as something that can speed all of that up.

And people who are not in GTM Ops are now using AI themselves, which gives them the impression that architecting and creating processes is easy.

I actually heard a great line this week on a podcast with Stephen Colbert: _“A little bit of information is a dangerous thing.”_ That applies perfectly here.

There are a lot of people on the GTM team who have that little bit of information, or a little experience with “operating,” and think they have the full picture. And some folks are getting comfortable executing with AI, but have very little ops experience to understand what it actually means to build a scalable system or foundation. They are building things because it is cool, not because it is useful.

If you have been in GTM Ops long enough, you know that having only a little bit of information or expertise in something means you actually know very little, because the more you know, the more you realize you do not. And I am not alone in this thinking.

Just this week, I saw [this post](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7447703227002880000?updateEntityUrn=urn%3Ali%3Afs_updateV2%3A%28urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7447703227002880000%2CFEED_DETAIL%2CEMPTY%2CDEFAULT%2Cfalse%29) by [Sara McNamara](https://www.linkedin.com/in/saramcnamara/) talking about the same thing. And I quickly hit the like button so others could see it.

## What We are Cookin' Up (for real)

This week is spring break at my first kid’s school, and some families, including a friend’s, had to cancel their trips to Hawaii due to a big storm coming in after they just had major flooding. Poor Hawaii can’t catch a break. 😭

Well in honor of Hawaii, or if you want to just feel transported there because you didn’t go anywhere for Spring Break, or if you too had to cancel your trip, then this meal idea is for you.

[Nina](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nina-valtcheva/) on our team shared to the foodies Slack channel a week ago a yummy at-home “poke-ish bowl” she made with items she got at Trader Joes.

Of course, you could use your own fresh tuna poke, or even buy some from a local store, but Nina actually diced up some of [TJ’s smoked tuna](https://www.traderjoes.com/home/products/pdp/sesame-crusted-hardwood-smoked-ahi-tuna-068227) into thick chunks to use for it. Genius! She also used their [Surimi (imitation crab)](https://www.traderjoes.com/home/products/pdp/surimi-imitation-crabmeat-081860) as well.

Such a fun at-home lunch or dinner idea! 🌺
